# Research: keeping players engaged at an intentional progression wall
*2026-08-03. Game-design evidence from auto-battlers, roguelites, idle and
gacha games. Full source links inline.*

## Case studies

**Darkest Dungeon.** Roster width is engineered: stress/afflictions force
rotation, town recovery sidelines heroes for weeks, so the B-team matters.
Heroes are individuated by accumulated damage (quirks, diseases, history),
which is why rotation reads as drama, not inventory. The Graveyard makes
loss a collection. Failure mode: "hamlet bloat" late-campaign (30 hours of
content in 60); rotation is loved when tactical, resented when calendar.
DD2 removed the persistent roster for run-based play and the community
immediately mourned the identity loop. (gamedeveloper.com "Encouraging the
Player to Have a Deep Bench"; thegemsbok.com critique; GDC 2016 Sigman
postmortem; DD2 Metacritic user reviews.)

**Slay the Spire Ascension.** 20 stacked, named, legible difficulty levels
per character. Wins ratchet up, losses never de-level. Balanced around ~A4
so A20 is a prestige summit. Daily Climb: seeded run + 3 modifiers +
leaderboard, no reward economy, pure shared puzzle. (slay-the-spire wiki;
Kotaku on Daily Climbs.)

**Hades.** Every death advances narrative (dialogue gated on attempts, not
clears); relationships progress between runs; post-win Heat is
player-composed difficulty per weapon. Failure is literally progress.
(itch.io "loops and arcs in Hades"; zleague Heat guide.)

**Monster Train / Dead Cells.** Covenant 25 is the intended difficulty you
are ramped toward; Boss Stem Cells raise difficulty AND rewards and are
freely removable (opt-in, never trapped).

**Rogue Legacy.** The death screen is a character-select screen: heirs with
comic genetic traits reframe loss as a fresh identity choice instantly.

**AFK Arena.** "The wall" is institutional; the wiki literally has a page
titled "What to do if you cannot progress the main story," answered by the
horizontal battery (King's Tower, Labyrinth, Peaks of Time, guild boss).
Campaign spikes deliberately redirect into endless modes. The Resonating
Crystal subsidizes roster width (top-5 set a level floor everyone inherits)
so teams 3-5 are nearly free. (deconstructoroffun.com AFK Arena teardown;
afk-arena.fandom.com.)

**Fate/Grand Order.** No PvP at all; story and a fixed annual event cycle
retain a small, intensely committed base. Narrative is wall-proof content.

**Arknights.** Integrated Strategies (permanent roguelike drafting from
your collection) converts width into replayability; Contingency Contract is
player-composed named risks, "respected because it rewards genuine roster
depth rather than pure stat scaling."

**HoYo Abyss/MoC.** Biweekly reset skill-checks work, but tuned lineups
became a powercreep sales channel and the community sees it. RNG gear
behind a resin timegate (Genshin artifacts) is the documented anti-pattern:
RNG times timegate multiplies resentment.

**What fails.** Daily-chore checklists ("second job" burnout), FOMO streaks
and expiring content (players quit permanently once they miss enough),
meta-currency that refills a designer-drained stat bar (reads as stalling),
grind padding.

## Design principles

Preparation loops:
1. The wall must redirect, never dead-end: 2-4 obvious alternative modes,
   each feeding resources back toward the wall.
2. Progress ratchets; failure costs only the attempt.
3. Let players author their own wall (named, legible, opt-in risk
   modifiers priced to reward).
4. Subsidize roster width at the wall (catch-up floors for teams 3-5).
5. Pay for skill, not just time; never multiply RNG by a timegate.
6. Meta-currency must widen options, not refill stats.

Identity loops:
7. Individuate units through accumulated history.
8. Memorialize everything; record-keeping itself creates the craving, and
   completion must be achievable (impossible collections churn
   completionists).
9. Gate story and relationships on ATTEMPTS, not clears.
10. Build modes that consume roster width so every acquisition is
    retroactively meaningful.

The line not to cross:
11. Cadence beats checklist: appointment-with-novelty (dailies leaderboard,
    league seasons) retains; obligation burns out.
12. Never punish absence; missed content should recur; time away should
    feel like preparation, not loss.

The unifying test: at the wall a player should be able to answer "what am I
building toward my next attempt?" and "what here is mine?", and never have
to answer "what do I owe the game today?"
