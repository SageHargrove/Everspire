# Research: what strong game landing pages actually do
*2026-08-03. Sites fetched directly: darkestdungeon.com, playbalatro.com,
inscryption.com, megacrit.com, persona.atlus.com/p5r, limbuscompany.com,
supergiantgames.com (Hades II), pathofexile.com. JS-locked (titles only,
pattern from prior knowledge): Genshin/HSR/ZZZ, Wuthering Waves, Arknights.*

## Per-site notes

**Darkest Dungeon (Red Hook).** Fold: logo plus one diegetic verb, "Enter."
No paragraph text. DD2 subsite: logo, store links (Epic/Steam/GOG/Humble/
consoles) ABOVE the trailer, then Buy Now. Structure: About, 3 full-width
feature blocks with evocative titles ("Deeper Battles, Darker Enemies"),
media gallery, 3 news posts, merch, community, newsletter, fan-art gallery.
Copy is 1-2 sentences per section, written in the Narrator's voice. The
character roster stands in the footer. ESRB/PEGI marks in the footer signal
"real shipped game."

**Balatro.** The whole page: logo, joker card art, ONE sentence, then 7+
platform badges. Nav is 4 items. Card suits are used as nav separators; the
live site runs the game's actual background shader. The site IS a Balatro
screen. Near-zero information architecture.

**Inscryption.** The site mimics a Windows desktop, continuous with the
game's meta-fiction. Essentially no text; icons and store links.

**Slay the Spire (Mega Crit).** Logo, key art, one-sentence pitch, one CTA.
Weakest identity of the indie set; plain sans-serif and generic blocks. The
useful contrast case: this is what "template" looks like even from a hit.

**Persona 5 Royal (Atlus).** Every UI element is a custom asset (headers,
buttons, decorative slashes are all PNGs). Character gallery with costume
toggles, School Life / Phantom Life sections, screenshots, then a stepped
purchase selector (version, platform, retailer). Almost no text. The page is
a scroll-through poster.

**Limbus Company.** Fold: logo, key-art carousel, Play CTA, store badges,
BGM toggle. The outlier on text volume (60-200 words/section) but ALL of it
is in-voice as a faux corporate onboarding manual with [REDACTED] blocks.
Long copy works only when the copy itself is an identity device.

**Hades II (Supergiant).** Logo, key art, 2 sentences, platform badges. Then:
review pull-quotes (three 10/10s), five trailers, 12-screenshot gallery,
merch. Proof over pitch; structurally conventional, carried by art and
third-party scores.

**Path of Exile.** The live-service pattern: the landing page is a news feed
and storefront for existing players (supporter packs, countdowns, deep nav).
Do NOT copy for a pre-launch page.

**HoYo template (from prior knowledge, verify visually).** Full-viewport
current-version key art or video, persistent floating download widget,
scroll cue, tabbed news, character showcase with full-body cutouts and
voice lines, media gallery, BGM toggle. The fold is versioned like a patch
banner; the page's job is "download now, here's the current banner."

## The 7 patterns that separate "official site" from "template with game words"

1. Fold is art + verb, not paragraph. One CTA, often diegetic ("Enter").
2. Platform/store badges appear early and repeat late; rating marks in the
   footer. Absence reads as vaporware.
3. The game's own assets ARE the page chrome (suits as separators, game
   buttons as web buttons, roster in the footer). CSS-styled generic
   components in the game's palette is the template tell.
4. All copy is in-voice or absent. Neutral marketing register never appears.
5. Proof over pitch: trailers, screenshot grids, review quotes fill the
   middle. Features are shown, not described.
6. Community/liveness at the bottom: 3 dated news items, Discord, newsletter.
   A page with no dated anything reads abandoned.
7. Sparse nav (4-7 items), single long scroll.

## For a menus-are-the-identity game (DD/P5/Balatro playbook)

- Make the site read as a screen of the game, so a UI screenshot placed on
  the page is visually continuous with the page around it.
- Website components as game furniture: your sigils as separators, your
  panel frames as cards, news styled as the in-game Herald.
- Show the menus as content, proudly; ideally a short autoplaying capture.
- Diegetic CTA verb ("Ascend").
- Character cutouts as a section unit.
- Optional: muted BGM toggle.

## Generic/AI tells to avoid

Paragraph above the fold; three-column icon+heading+blurb feature cards;
neutral register ("embark on an epic journey"); styled-default rounded
buttons; no badges or rating marks; perfectly even section rhythm; no dated
news; describing instead of showing.
