# Research: Pick Me Up, Infinite Gacha — what masters do between floor pushes
*2026-08-03. Sources: the 7-page Novel Updates Forum spoiler thread and the
two Fandom wikis (infinite-gacha.fandom.com, pick-me-up.fandom.com).
namu.wiki's settings page is captcha-walled and remains untapped. Forum
numbers are fan-recalled; treat as approximate.*

Verified names: Earth protagonist Han Seojin, master account "Loki", world
rank 5. Transmigrates into his own game as 1-star hero Han Yslat. His old
lobby: Niflheim. The newbie lobby he lands in: Taonier, run by master Anytng.

## 1. Lobby / base management
- The lobby ("waiting room") is a persistent hub where summoned heroes live.
  Taonier's layout: floor 1 combatants/foragers, floor 2 prospects, floor 3
  mains. Facilities: training grounds, hall of magic, storage, synthesis
  center, promotion station, equipment shop, engraving station, stables,
  later a hangar (airships) and a Tactical Center so the master herself can
  join tactics.
- **Facility unlocks are gated by tower progress: every 10 floors conquered
  unlocks new facility types.** Upgrades cost materials, craftsmen, and real
  construction time.
- Niflheim (endgame lobby): 13 floors, capacity 200,000, buildings level
  18-22. The base is the visible embodiment of years of play.
- Cosmetic morale economy: banquet food, pajamas, glow sticks to cheer
  heroes during missions. 13 built-in minigames tied to facilities (e.g. a
  blacksmithing minigame where the MASTER's skill improves output).
- The game world runs at 3x Earth speed; heroes live whole lives between
  logins.

## 2. Hero training (the F2P-knowledge engine)
- Stated doctrine: **training refines skills; levels come only from combat.**
  Low-star heroes are born classless with hidden talents the master must
  discover.
- The meta treats 1-stars as disposable pattern-scouts. Loki's heresy: he
  refused synthesis, never pulled above one natural 4-star, and nurtured
  low-stars with potential. His 4-star-born units became some of the
  strongest 6-stars in the game; world rank 5 without a natural 5-star.
  "A lobby controlled by fear is bound to fall."
- Infrastructure: scheduled named instructors, private practice rooms,
  sparring, an AI-driven autonomous training mode.
- **Replay stones**: recordings of past runs that rookies study to pre-learn
  boss patterns. The in-fiction "watching the guide video."
- Paid external training: elite lobbies sell 10-day programs for gems;
  heroes can be loaned between lobbies.
- **Engravings**: equipable innate-ability slots that upgrade on star
  promotion (S to SS unlocks new abilities). Build identity.

## 3. Inter-master systems
- Servers, and sectors of ~100 masters reshuffled every 10 floors.
- Global rankings (El Cid is world 1 and the only 7-star holder), event
  leaderboards with point totals.
- The multiplayer layer unlocks at master level 20+ and includes invasions:
  opting into social play is opting into risk.
- PVP over "ruins" (capture-and-hold) pays exclusive materials. Past hero
  level 40, PVP deaths become permanent.
- Guilds range from decorator co-ops to server-domination cartels.
- **MyuTube**: masters stream/post clear videos, gain subscribers, get
  flamed; watching other masters' clears is the canonical strategy-spying
  channel. Viral videos yield in-game rewards.

## 4. Floor farming
- Daily dungeons rotate by weekday and are the material/XP loop.
- Canonical grind: repeat-clear the highest safe floor to bank XP toward
  promotion thresholds (level 55 for 5-star, 70 for 6-star).
- Floor payouts scale steeply (floor 55: 100k gold; floor 60: 300k; a
  floor-8x boss: 10M).
- Advent Dungeons: optional side dungeons with tiered/unknown difficulty.

## 5. Gacha economics in-story
- Two tracks: gold summons (1-3 star) and gem summons (3-5 star). Summon
  probabilities vary per account (an in-fiction luck stat).
- **Guarantees are achievement-gated, not pity-counted**: "5-Star Confirmed
  Ticket" from event victories; newbie advanced tickets. Earned trophies
  with provenance.
- Star culture: community dogma says the last walls need natural 5-stars;
  7-star is quasi-mythical. Synthesis is deliberately lossy (a 100-power
  sacrifice grants ~10) and is morally a killing; Loki routes around it.

## 6. Heroes, death, permadeath
- Heroes are real abducted people, not AI. Death in battle or by synthesis
  is forever. Synthesis damages lobby morale and survivors' sanity.
- Memory rules: low-stars arrive with home-world memories suppressed;
  high-stars remember. Heroes learn the truth as they climb.
- Memorials are concrete: keepsake storage, a cemetery with tombstones,
  casualty ledgers ("15 went, 8 came back").
- Loyalty is earned; heroes argue and sometimes disobey. Late reveal: a
  hero's power literally scales with the master-hero bond, so kindness is
  mechanically optimal.

## 7. The tower
- 100 floors, each a timed mission with a type (exploration, extermination,
  siege, escort, escape, rescue) that can mutate mid-run.
- **The Wailing Wall (floor 80)**: an ordered-objective knowledge check, not
  a stat check; only five masters in history cleared floor 79.
- Endgame reveal: floors above 90 were never implemented; the game is an
  unfinished divine emergency system. (Fiction only, but the wall-as-myth
  framing is the marketing lesson.)

## Top 10 translatable systems (ranked for Giltgrave)
1. Facility unlocks gated by tower checkpoints (every 10 floors).
2. Training refines skills, combat grants levels; talents on low-stars are
   discovered, not shown.
3. Replay stones: wipes convert into drillable pattern knowledge.
4. Wall floors as ordered-objective knowledge checks.
5. Casualty ledger + memorial keepsakes auto-generated from hero history.
6. Bond = power (the "interference" reveal), built by gifts and honesty.
7. Lossy synthesis as a moral economy (fast-but-corrosive vs slow-but-loyal).
8. Achievement-gated guarantees with provenance instead of pity counters.
9. Clear videos / ghost replays as social objects and fame currency.
10. Highest-safe-floor farming with visible promotion finish lines.
